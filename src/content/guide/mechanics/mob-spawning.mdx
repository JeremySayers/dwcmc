## Where a hostile mob can spawn

For a hostile mob to spawn on a block, all of these have to be true:

- **It's dark.** The light level where the mob's feet would be is 7 or lower. The darker it is, the more likely a spawn.
- **It's not too close to you.** Nothing spawns within 24 blocks of a player.
- **It's not too far from you.** Mobs only spawn in the area loaded around a player, about 128 blocks out in every direction (8 chunks each way).
- **There's a solid block underneath** and the mob isn't in water.
- **The mob fits.** The space above the floor has to be big enough for that mob.

| Mob                         | Floor space | Headroom |
| --------------------------- | ----------- | -------- |
| Zombie, skeleton, creeper   | 1 × 1       | 2 blocks |
| Spider                      | 3 × 3       | 1 block  |
| Enderman                    | 1 × 1       | 3 blocks |
| Slime (smallest; see below) | 1 × 1       | 1 block  |

That table is the basis of most farm tricks. A room with a 2-block ceiling can't spawn endermen.

Spiders are the odd one out. A spider is 1.4 blocks wide and spawns centred on a block, so it needs the whole 3 × 3 area around that block to be clear. One block, or even a slab, in the middle of a 3 × 3 pad stops spiders spawning on any of the 8 blocks around it. That's the trick in [Etho's mob system](/farms/ethos-mob-system/).

<Unconfirmed basis="Seen in 1.2 and Beta 1.8.1 code, not checked in 1.1">
  Exactly which blocks count as a valid floor (slabs, glass, trapdoors and so on), and some mob hitbox sizes.
  Test a floor block in a creative copy with spawn eggs before building a whole farm on it.
</Unconfirmed>

## The mob cap

There's a limit on how many hostile mobs can exist at once around you. For one player it's about **79**: the base cap of 70, scaled by the number of loaded chunks (17 × 17 = 289 chunks, so 70 × 289 ÷ 256).

The cap counts **every** loaded hostile mob, not only the ones in your farm. A zombie standing in a dark cave 100 blocks away takes a slot your farm could have used. That's why lighting matters so much for farm rates: every dark cave within about 128 blocks competes with your spawning pads.

To free up the cap, light every cave within that range, fill them in, or flood them. Nothing spawns in water.

## Slimes

Slimes play by different rules. They spawn in **slime chunks**, about 1 in 10 chunks, chosen by the world seed, and only **below Y 40**. Light doesn't stop them.

Slimes, including the small ones a big slime splits into, **count towards the hostile cap**. A slime chunk with open caves below Y 40 near your farm can quietly fill part of it, and torches won't help.

- **Flood it.** Nothing spawns in water.
- **Fill it in.** Small slimes fit in 1-block gaps, so any air above solid floor below Y 40 in a slime chunk can spawn them.
- **Find the chunks first.** You can't see slime chunks in the game, but slimes wandering through a lit cave below Y 40 give them away. Only those chunks need dealing with.

Slimes despawn by the same distance rules as other hostile mobs, but while one exists it takes a cap slot.

## Despawning

How far a mob is from the nearest player decides whether it can despawn:

- **More than 128 blocks away:** it's removed straight away.
- **Between 32 and 128 blocks away:** once it has gone about 30 seconds without a player within 32 blocks, it has a small random chance to despawn every tick, so far-off mobs disappear over the following minute or so.
- **Within 32 blocks:** it never despawns.

<Unconfirmed basis="EthosLab (August 2012) says this was added 'a few patches ago'">
  Mobs more than 32 blocks from a player can go idle and stop moving until a player comes back within 32
  blocks. Etho describes this as a recent change in 2012, so it may not exist in 1.1 yet. If it does, mobs on
  a pad more than 32 blocks away won't walk into your water channels at all.
</Unconfirmed>

## How mobs move

A newly spawned mob wanders at random. It isn't looking for anything in particular unless it notices a player, so whether it ends up in your collection system is down to where it spawned and how much it wanders.

<Unconfirmed basis="Seen in 1.2 and Beta 1.8.1 code, not checked in 1.1">
  Mobs only wander at random for about the first 5 seconds after they spawn. That would explain why big open
  spawning floors work poorly in 1.1, and why every spawn spot in a good farm is only a step or two from
  water.
</Unconfirmed>

**In water**, mobs jump about 80% of the time, so they bob at the surface instead of sinking, and flowing water carries them along. A mob in a water channel goes wherever the current goes. That's what makes the classic designs work: spawn mobs next to water, and let the water do the rest.

## Lighting tunnels and caves

A torch gives light level 14, and light drops by 1 for every block it travels. Mobs can spawn at light 7 or below, so every floor block needs a light level of at least 8.

In a straight tunnel 1 block wide and 2 blocks high:

- **Torch at foot level** (on the floor, or low on the wall): the floor stays at light 8 or more for 6 blocks each way. Place a torch **every 13 blocks**.
- **Torch at head height** (high on the wall): the floor below it starts at 13, so it covers 5 blocks each way. Place a torch **every 11 blocks**.

A torch every 10 blocks is easy to count while you're mining and leaves a margin.

On a **staircase**, light loses 2 levels per step, because it travels one block across and one down. A torch on a step covers 3 steps each way, so place one at least every 7 steps. Every 5 or 6 is safer.

Only zombies, skeletons and creepers can spawn in a 1 × 2 tunnel. Spiders are too wide and endermen are too tall.
