## The levels on the buttons

Put an item in the table and it offers three buttons, each with a green number from 1 to 50. That number is the **table level**, and it's exactly how many experience levels you pay.

The text on the buttons is random decoration in the "standard galactic alphabet". It says nothing about what you'll get.

## Bookshelves

Bookshelves raise the levels on offer. A shelf counts when it's two blocks out from the table, level with it or one block higher, with nothing in between. A full ring is 15 shelves on each of two layers, with one gap to walk in: 30 shelves in all.

| Bookshelves | Top button | Middle button | Bottom button |
| ----------: | ---------: | ------------: | ------------: |
|           0 |        1–3 |           1–4 |           1–5 |
|          10 |       4–11 |          5–14 |          6–20 |
|          20 |       6–18 |          8–24 |         11–35 |
|          30 |       9–26 |         11–34 |         16–50 |

With all 30 shelves, the bottom button lands on exactly 50 only about once in every 150 offers. Most of the time it shows something in the 30s or 40s, which is often the better level anyway (see [why a level can be too high](#why-a-level-can-be-too-high)).

To get lower offers, put a torch in the gap between a shelf and the table. It switches that shelf off without breaking anything.

## The hidden modified level

The game never uses the table level directly. It first turns it into a hidden **modified level**:

1. **It adds a material bonus.** Each material has an _enchantability_: diamond 10, gold 22 for tools and 25 for armour, a bow 1. The game rolls two random numbers from 0 to half the enchantability and adds 1 plus both of them to the level. A diamond item gets +1 to +11, most often around +6. A bow always gets exactly +1.
2. **It scales the result by up to ±25%.** It multiplies by a random factor between 0.75 and 1.25, usually close to 1, and rounds to the nearest whole number.

```
bonus = 1 + rand(0..E/2) + rand(0..E/2)
M     = round((L + bonus) × (1 + rand(−0.25..0.25)))
```

So a diamond pickaxe at table level 30 can end up with a modified level anywhere from 23 to 51, but 90% of rolls land between 29 and 43, most often around 35–36.

## Which tiers are possible

Each tier of each enchantment has a **window** of modified levels where it can appear. Silk Touch's window is 25–61. Fortune I is 20–61, Fortune II is 32–71 and Fortune III is 44–81.

For every enchantment the item can take, the game checks the tiers from I upwards and keeps the highest tier whose window contains the modified level. If none match, that enchantment can't appear on this roll. At a modified level of 50, Fortune can only be Fortune III, never I or II.

## Weights and bonus enchantments

Once the game knows which enchantments are possible, it picks one at random, weighted. Common enchantments (weight 10: Protection, Sharpness, Efficiency, Power) are ten times as likely as rare ones (weight 1: Silk Touch, Infinity). Silk Touch competes with Efficiency (10), Unbreaking (5) and Fortune (2), which is why it's never better than about 1 in 8.

You always get at least one enchantment. The game may then add more: with probability (M/2 + 1)/50 it adds another, first removing anything incompatible. Silk Touch removes Fortune, for example, and each protection type removes the others. Each time it adds one, the chance of another halves. A high modified level gives more bonus enchantments, but they come from the same weighted pool, so they barely raise the odds of a rare one.

Every enchantment's tier was already fixed by the full modified level, so bonus enchantments don't come in at lower tiers.

## Bows

<AppliesTo versions={['1.1', '1.2']} note="Bows can't be enchanted in 1.0." />

1.1 added four bow enchantments: Power (up to V), Punch (up to II), Flame and Infinity. A bow's enchantability is 1, so its modified level is always the table level plus 1, scaled by up to ±25%.

Infinity and Flame both stop at modified level 50, so they peak around table level 40–41: Infinity at about 16% and Flame at about 29%. At level 50 a bow comes out with nothing about 12% of the time. You aren't charged, but the roll is wasted.

## Paying for it

Before 1.3, going from level _k_ to _k_ + 1 costs `7 + ⌊3.5k⌋` XP. Most hostile mobs drop about 5 XP; blazes drop 10.

| From level 0 to | Total XP | Zombies (5 XP) | Blazes (10 XP) |
| --------------: | -------: | -------------: | -------------: |
|              10 |      225 |             45 |             23 |
|              20 |      800 |            160 |             80 |
|              30 |    1,725 |            345 |            173 |
|              40 |    3,000 |            600 |            300 |
|              50 |    4,625 |            925 |            463 |

Enchanting takes away levels, not XP, and each level costs more than the last. Enchanting at 30 from level 30 costs 1,725 XP, but enchanting at 30 from level 50 costs 3,825 XP, because you lose your most expensive levels. Enchant as soon as you reach the level you want.

## Other things worth knowing

- You can only enchant an item once. There are no anvils and no enchanted books, so you can't combine or repair enchantments.
- Only helmets, chestplates, leggings, boots, swords, pickaxes, axes, shovels and bows can be enchanted. Hoes, shears and fishing rods can't.
- Swords and bows can't get Unbreaking. There is no Thorns.

## What changed in 1.3

1.3 capped the table at level 30 with 15 bookshelves, changed the level and window formulas and the XP curve, and made mining ores and smelting give experience. None of that applies to 1.0–1.2. The [Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Enchanting_mechanics) covers the later rules.
