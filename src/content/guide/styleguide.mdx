This page isn't in the sidebar. It shows every component a guide page can use. Import them at the top of an MDX file:

```mdx
import { Callout, Item, ItemList } from '@/components/ui';
```

## Text

Body text, **bold**, _italic_, `inline code` and [links](/). Headings `##` and `###` appear in the "On this page" list on the right.

> A blockquote, for a quote from the game or a changelog.

## Variants

Switches between alternative versions of some content. Good for comparing drafts of copy, or two ways to build the same farm. The reader's choice is remembered.

```mdx
<Variants id="intro" label="Intro draft">
  <Variant name="Personal" note="First person.">
    ...
  </Variant>
  <Variant name="Short">...</Variant>
</Variants>
```

<Variants id="styleguide-intro" label="Intro draft" footnote="These are the drafts the home page was chosen from.">
  <Variant name="Build with what you have" note="Earnest, medium length.">

For a lot of us, Minecraft was never about having every block. It was about looking at what you had and working out what you could make with it. A cobblestone wall, a few torches, a water channel and a lot of stubbornness could become a castle, a mob farm or a railway across the map.

  </Variant>
  <Variant name="Short and punchy" note="Easiest to skim; least personal.">

Minecraft has always been a game about limits. You get a handful of blocks and a world that doesn't care, and your imagination does the rest.

  </Variant>
  <Variant name="Personal" note="First person. This one is on the home page.">

Newer versions of Minecraft have more of everything. More blocks, more mobs, more systems to learn. For me, more of everything is part of the problem.

  </Variant>
  <Variant name="Edges of the sandbox" note="Frames the limits as the point.">

A sandbox is interesting because of where it stops. Minecraft 1.1 has clear edges: one kind of plank, no way to pipe items around, no villager shops, a world 128 blocks tall.

  </Variant>
</Variants>

## Applies to

Which versions a page or section holds for. Every chip is always shown, so you can see where a rule starts and stops. Article pages get one under the title from the `versions` frontmatter; put one straight after a heading when a section is different.

```mdx
<AppliesTo versions={['1.1', '1.2']} note="Bows can't be enchanted in 1.0." />
```

<AppliesTo versions={['1.0', '1.1', '1.2']} />

<AppliesTo versions={['1.1', '1.2']} note="Bows can't be enchanted in 1.0." />

<AppliesTo
  versions={['b1.8', '1.0', '1.1', '1.2', '1.3', '1.4+']}
  label="Works in"
  note="A farm that works in every version."
/>

## Callout

Styled like an item tooltip. Use it for the one thing on a page the reader shouldn't miss.

```mdx
<Callout icon="lamp" title="What 1.1 gives up" subtitle="Redstone lamp · added in 1.2">
  Text goes here.
</Callout>
```

<Callout icon="lamp" title="What 1.1 gives up" subtitle="Redstone lamp · added in 1.2">
  A light you can switch on and off is the one thing from 1.2 that's hard to live without.
</Callout>

<Callout title="No icon">The icon and subtitle are optional.</Callout>

## Unconfirmed

For a claim that hasn't been checked in 1.1 yet. Say where it comes from. Remove the box once the claim is tested, and add the source to the page.

```mdx
<Unconfirmed basis="Seen in 1.2 code, not checked in 1.1">
  Mobs only wander at random for about their first 5 seconds.
</Unconfirmed>
```

<Unconfirmed basis="Seen in 1.2 code, not checked in 1.1">
  Mobs only wander at random for about their first 5 seconds.
</Unconfirmed>

## Item list

An icon in a slot, a title and a line of text. Two columns by default; `columns={1}` for longer text. `tag` marks the version something arrived in.

```mdx
<ItemList>
  <Item icon="wool" title="Sheep regrow their wool">
    Text goes here.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="golem" title="Iron golems" tag="1.2">
    Text goes here.
  </Item>
</ItemList>
```

<ItemList>
  <Item icon="wool" title="Sheep regrow their wool">
    A sheared sheep grows its wool back by eating grass.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="golem" title="Iron golems" tag="1.2">
    Not in 1.1. Villages spawn golems that drop iron.
  </Item>
</ItemList>

## Layer diagram

Top-down maps of a build, one per block of height, bottom first, with a legend and a count of what you need. Each layer is a list of strings, one character per block. The characters are defined in `src/lib/blocks.ts`. For a real build, keep the layers in `src/lib/designs/`, as the Etho mob system page does.

```mdx
<LayerDiagram label="A single spawning pad" layers={[{ name: 'Floor', rows: ['#####', ...] }, ...]} />
```

## Buttons

`<Button href="...">` is a link. Without `href` it shows as disabled, for things that aren't ready.

<Actions>
  <Button href="/">A link</Button>
  <Button>Not ready yet</Button>
</Actions>
