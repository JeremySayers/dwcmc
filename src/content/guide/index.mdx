# Why I keep coming back to 1.1.

Newer versions of Minecraft have more of everything. More blocks, more mobs, more systems to learn. For me, more of everything is part of the problem.

Minecraft has always been about working with what you have. When oak planks, cobblestone and glass are most of your palette, you stop scrolling through options and start solving problems. A farm becomes a puzzle you work out yourself, not a design you copy because a newer block makes it trivial.

1.1 is a simpler game. You can hold every mechanic in your head at once. I'm not much of an artist, and a short block list means I can still build something I'm proud of. This site is everything I've learned about playing it: the mechanics, the farms and the builds.

## Why this site exists

<p class="lede">Accurate information about 1.1 is surprisingly hard to find.</p>

Minecraft has changed so much since 2012 that almost everything written about it today describes a different game. Search for how enchanting works and you get the 1.3 rules. Search for an iron farm and you get a mob that 1.1 doesn't have. The wikis describe the current version, and the 1.1 details are buried in history notes, if they made it in at all.

Working out how something really behaved in 1.1 has meant digging through old forum threads, scrubbing through Let's Plays recorded at the time, and sometimes reading the game's code. I wanted all of that in one place, checked, for anyone else who wants to try playing older Minecraft.

### Before it disappears

It's also about keeping this information around. The places that hold it are disappearing:

- Forums shut down, or survive without the images that made their posts make sense.
- Farm designs from 2012 link to image hosts and download sites that died years ago.
- Wiki pages get rewritten for the current version, and older details drop out.
- Videos get deleted, made private, or vanish with the channels that posted them.

Once they're gone, nobody can check how the game used to work. This site is my attempt to write it down while it can still be checked.

### What each page covers

Most of 1.1 works the same in 1.0 or 1.2, and some farms work in any version. So every page shows which versions it applies to, like this:

## Why 1.1?

  Every version of Minecraft is somebody's favourite. This guide picks 1.1 on purpose, and stops there on

### Why not Beta or 1.0

Beta 1.8 had no enchanting, no brewing and no End. 1.0 added all three in November 2011 and made Minecraft a finished game. Two months later, 1.1 fixed the things that still felt unfinished:

<ItemList>
  <Item icon="wool" title="Sheep regrow their wool">
    A sheared sheep grows its wool back by eating grass. A small pen is a wool farm.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="beach" title="Beaches">
    Sand and sandstone between land and ocean, so coastlines look the way you expect.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="apple" title="Apples from oak leaves">
    One in 200 oak leaves drops an apple, so golden apples are something you can actually make.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="bow" title="Bow enchantments">
    Power, Punch, Flame and Infinity. Bows get a reason to visit the enchanting table.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="melon" title="Melons and pumpkins on dirt">
    Stems can grow fruit onto plain dirt, not only farmland.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="gate" title="Fence gates with redstone">
    Gates open and close from a lever, button or pressure plate.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="disc" title="Music discs you can find">
    Eight discs drop when a skeleton's arrow kills a creeper.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="egg" title="Superflat and spawn eggs">
    A flat world type and creative-mode spawn eggs for testing farms and builds.
  </Item>
</ItemList>

### Why stop before 1.2

1.2 is a good update, and it's the closest call. It also starts to change the core loops that make 1.1 work.

<ItemList columns={1}>
  <Item icon="golem" title="Iron golems" tag="1.2">
    Villages spawn golems that drop iron, and you can build your own. Once iron comes from a village, mining
    for it starts to feel optional.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="planks" title="A lot more blocks" tag="1.2">
    Spruce, birch and jungle planks, cut and chiseled sandstone, chiseled stone bricks. Great if you have a
    strong eye for design. For everyone else, more options means more second-guessing and less building.
  </Item>
</ItemList>

<Callout icon="lamp" title="What 1.1 gives up" subtitle="Redstone lamp · added in 1.2">
  A light you can switch on and off is the one thing from 1.2 that's hard to live without. The guide covers
  the 1.1 workarounds, like pistons that slide glowstone in and out of a wall.
</Callout>

### Why not 1.3

<ItemList columns={1}>
  <Item icon="emerald" title="Villager trading" tag="1.3">
    Emeralds buy diamond gear, food and more, so one good village can stand in for mining, farming and
    fighting. It turns exploring into shopping.
  </Item>
  <Item icon="book" title="New enchanting rules" tag="1.3">
    The highest level dropped from 50 to 30, bookshelves from 30 to 15, and the XP curve changed. The
    calculators on this site only make sense for 1.1.
  </Item>
</ItemList>
