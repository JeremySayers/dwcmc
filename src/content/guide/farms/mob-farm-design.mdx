<p class="lede">
  The earliest mob farms were big dark floors with trapdoors over water, and they mostly worked. The better
  ones from the same era used small 3 × 3 pads instead. Here's why, and how to design your own.
</p>

These rules come from [hostile mob spawning](/mechanics/mob-spawning/) and [fall damage](/mechanics/fall-damage/). For a complete design that uses all of them, see [Etho's practical mob system](/farms/ethos-mob-system/).

## The short version

- Build **small, completely dark pads** surrounded by **water channels**, not big open floors.
- Keep every spawn spot **a step or two from water**.
- Give the pads a ceiling **exactly 2 blocks high**, so endermen can't spawn.
- Put a **slab or block in the middle of each 3 × 3 pad**, so spiders can't spawn.
- Stand **24 to 32 blocks** from every pad block while you wait.
- **Light, fill or flood every cave within about 128 blocks**, and deal with slime chunks below Y 40.
- Drop the mobs **22 blocks** for one-hit kills, or **into water** to bring them down unhurt.

## Small pads beat big floors

Mobs only reach your water by wandering into it, and they don't seem to wander for long (see below). On a big floor, most spawns stop moving long before they reach water and just stand there taking up the cap. On a 3 × 3 pad ringed by water, every spawn spot is one or two steps from a channel, so mobs fall in almost straight away.

## Keeping spiders and endermen out

Spiders need a clear 3 × 3 area around the block they spawn on, and endermen need 3 blocks of headroom.

- **Spiders:** a spider is 1.4 blocks wide and spawns centred on a block, so the whole 3 × 3 area around that block has to be clear. Put a stone slab on the middle block of each 3 × 3 pad: it sits inside that area for all 8 blocks around it. A wall beside a spawning block stops spiders the same way.
- **Endermen:** make the ceiling over every pad exactly 2 blocks high. Zombies, skeletons and creepers still fit.

## Where to stand

Nothing spawns within 24 blocks of you. Mobs more than 32 blocks away start to despawn, and may stop moving. So the best spot is **24 to 32 blocks from every pad block**. Measure it out before you build, because it limits how big the spawning area can be.

## Starve every other dark space

The hostile cap is about 79 for one player, and it counts every hostile mob that's loaded, including ones standing in caves you've never visited. Every one of them is a slot your farm can't use.

- **Light** every cave within about 128 blocks of where you'll stand. In tunnels, a torch every 10 blocks is enough.
- **Fill in or flood** caves you don't want to light. Nothing spawns in water.
- **Slimes** ignore light. In slime chunks below Y 40 they spawn in any small gap and count towards the cap. Flood or fill those spaces.

## Moving and killing the mobs

Water channels carry the mobs to a drop. From there:

- **For one-hit kills**, drop them 22 blocks onto solid ground. Zombies, skeletons and creepers land on one hit point. This is also how the classic farms got XP from mobs: you get it when you land the final hit.
- **To bring them down unhurt** (for example to a lower collection level), land them in water. A landing in water cancels any fall.
